2. Direct allocation of the initial deposit: after the minimum and deposit amount are discussed, B says, “Make sure to put $150 in each account.”

Do not label it complete account-opening competence or generate TTS and count it as screened human input.

## 2. Renting — ask costs and deposit conditions before deciding

**Publisher/source:** Randall's ESL Cyber Listening Lab, [Great Apartment Living](https://www.esl-lab.com/intermediate/apartment-living/), with [publisher script](https://www.esl-lab.com/intermediate/apartment-living-script/). **Actual acts:**

2. Clarify up-front cost and return conditions: caller asks, “Oh, and how much is the deposit?” The next exchange questions whether it is returned on departure; owner describes deductions.

**Media/timing:** activity lists **03:46**, Man–Woman, and [this MP3](https://esllab.b-cdn.net/audio/mp3/apartment.mp3). These are publisher metadata/links, not playback or measured-duration evidence. Selected start/end and turn alignment: **unknown**.

**Rights:** R1, blocked before download/storage/STT/app import. **Voice:** human origin, accent, naturalness, clarity and inserted material unknown. If permission is obtained, inspect the cost/lease section as a possible coherent interval.

**Review boundary:** a comic, deteriorating property and questionable deposit assertions are not current tenancy law or a recommended landlord practice. This is inquiry language, not a completed lease negotiation. Keep separate from the next packet's existing-tenant complaint.

**Media/timing:** activity lists **01:40**, Man–Man, and [this MP3](https://esllab.b-cdn.net/audio/mp3/complain.mp3). No verified subinterval or turn times.

**Review boundary:** complaint/escalation language, not evidence of successful resolution, a repair booking, tenancy rights or an acceptable response time. Repair topics in the page's separate investigation exercise are **not spoken dialogue evidence**. Preserve the distinction when choosing task tags.
